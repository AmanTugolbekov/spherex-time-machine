        How it works

        SPHEREx is a NASA space telescope launched in 2025. Every six months it maps the entire sky in 102 colors of near-infrared light, from 0.75 to 5 µm. Each place on the sky therefore gets photographed again and again.

        This tool fetches every SPHEREx exposure that covers the spot you pick and lines them up pixel by pixel. Things that stay put (most stars and galaxies) look identical. Things that move or change brightness jump out:

        • Blink flips between two images. Your eye spots anything that jumps. Clyde Tombaugh found Pluto this way in 1930.
        • Swipe drags a curtain between the two.
        • Difference subtracts A from B. Steady objects cancel out. green is brighter in B, blue is brighter in A. An asteroid shows up as a blue–green pair.
        • Movie plays one exposure from each six-month sky survey.

        The six wavelength buttons are SPHEREx's six detectors. Shorter wavelengths see starlight. Longer ones see warm dust, ices and cool brown dwarfs.

        Data: SPHEREx Quick Release, NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive (IRSA). Known small bodies: JPL Small-Body Identification API. Background maps: CDS Aladin Lite / HiPS.
